# Goblin Stronghold — next-session handoff

Updated: 2026-08-30

## Verified boundary

- Active branch: `master`.
- The working tree is intentionally dirty across the accumulated playable-prototype work. It contains both modified and new source, test, documentation and asset files. Do not reset, restore or remove unrelated changes when continuing.
- Save format 73 is the current pre-release compatibility baseline. It pins the stable `core:demo-swamp-frontier` location-profile ID and the selected absent/single/branching river mode alongside the seed, generator version, dimensions and immutable-map fingerprint. Format-72 saves migrate to the former single-channel river; format-71 saves additionally migrate to the default profile, and format-70 saves also migrate forward with empty surface/carried grime for goblins, human villagers and animals. All three remain visible in the title/recovery menus; earlier formats, unknown profiles or river modes, and incompatible map-generator versions are rejected. Map generator version is 14 and the new-game default is 96×96.
- Continue now selects the highest authoritative tick from the most recently written world rather than blindly preferring `quicksave.json`. The title menu identifies the selected file/tick and exposes an exact-slot recovery picker. A verified rotating pre-load recovery copy protects the active session before replacement.
- Format-69 loading retains the loose-surface-stack repair introduced at the previous baseline: invalid legacy-column positions are projected onto the real material surface or a deterministic nearby stable flat cell, while already valid coordinates, including mined resources on underground ramps, remain unchanged. Future felled-wood scattering uses the same resolver. Load failures open an explicit title-safe dialog rather than writing only to the hidden in-game inspector. Earlier user saves were verified before the exact baseline advanced and are now intentionally obsolete.
- Standing tree trunks, large boulders and dead tree stumps are solid navigation obstacles on the column's real material surface. Legacy natural-object occupancy anchored at `z=0` is projected onto both raised and depressed terrain, so goblins, animals and path queries cannot pass through those objects until the tree is felled, the boulder is quarried or the stump is harvested.
- Tree layers now have a dedicated illustrated 2x2 atlas: standing trunks, cut stumps, exposed underground roots, and felled remains (one log section with scattered branches). Freshly felled trees retain the decorative remains beside their stump until the stump is harvested; naturally generated old stumps do not receive fresh debris.
- The deterministic oak, chestnut, birch, walnut, apple and pine identity used by felled wood now also tints the source tree's standing trunk, underground roots, cut stump and felled debris in both renderers. The shared position-based policy keeps presentation and physical output aligned without adding mutable save state.
- Cell inspection names each tree species and distinguishes its currently visible crown, standing trunk, underground roots, old stump, or fresh stump with felled log and branches. Natural-object inspection uses the same surface-height projection as rendering, including raised and depressed columns.
- Living oak, chestnut, birch, walnut, apple and pine trees now use six authored overhead crown silhouettes instead of one generic broadleaf crown. The 3×2 transparent atlas follows the same deterministic species policy as trunks and felled output; the experimental 3D crown colors follow that identity too.
- `Simulation/Content/materials.json` schema 2 is the authoritative typed catalog for woods, reeds, bone, hide, rocks, coal, ores, gems and metal bars. Shared properties cover weight, strength, hardness, durability, flexibility, value, difficulty and palette. Optional natural occurrence is separate from acquisition strategy; acquisition defines tool kind/level, skill level and work multiplier, while processed materials define their processor, level, inputs and yield. Supported-use tags enable recipes to request any suitable construction, furniture, tool, weapon, armor, container, decoration or sculpture material. Iron bars use a bloomery, copper a smelting furnace, and silver/gold a level-2 crucible furnace. Rock mining reads its requirements and duration multiplier from this catalog, while Godot reads its material colors.
- `Simulation/Content/workshops.json` schema 1 defines primitive workshop, bloomery, smelting furnace and crucible furnace levels, construction-material thresholds, work-speed percentages, maximum recipe levels, available ordinary recipes and served material outputs. Construction and crafting enforce the configured material thresholds, recipe availability, level and speed. All four workshops are physical buildings with dispatcher jobs, and the three furnaces validate every ore-to-bar processing contract.
- `Simulation/Content/crafting-recipes.json` schema 1 is the authoritative catalog for recipe identity, owning workshop, level, base duration, resource/variant quantities and physical output variant. Crafting planning, reservations, delivery, snapshots, hashing and format-70 saves preserve the concrete delivered variant while broad requirements can still accept any valid wood or stone. Iron, copper, silver and gold smelting contracts require their exact ore plus coal and are cross-checked against the processor, level, inputs and yield in `materials.json`.
- Bloomery, smelting furnace and crucible furnace are physical, level-independent construction blueprints and executable workshops. Their stone quantities and strength/durability filters come from `workshops.json`; the bloomery accepts sandstone, while the later furnaces require stronger stone. Builders need a pickaxe and Building level 1 or 2. The Godot build menu, 2D/3D rendering, inspection and recipe window distinguish all workshop types and show only their supported recipes. Completed processing leaves a physical metal-bar stack, and metal bars are valid saveable/storeable materials.
- Durable constructions lock to the concrete wood or stone variant delivered first, require the remainder to match, persist that identity through format-70 save/load, and transfer it to the completed world object. Workshops, illustrated huts/camps, connected walls, frames, doors, torches and ordinary 2D/3D structure geometry use the authoritative four-shade material palette. A cached texture mapper replaces the complete connected-wall surface and selectively replaces warm timber in composite hut/camp illustrations, preserving their cloth, bone, stone, dirt and fire details. Natural boulders reuse the authored neutral gray stone cluster from the item atlas as their keyed master, preserving its silhouette and facets while deriving the visible rock type from the same palette catalog. Each connected barrier retains its own world-object snapshot so neighboring segments can visibly use different materials. Abstract storage-zone and container-placement requests intentionally accept mixed construction inputs because their output is a capacity provider rather than a material-bearing world object.
- Format 66 introduced multiple authoritative logistics networks, format 67 added storage-area authority and format 68 physical solid containers. Format 69 added deterministic management commands, the consolidated Godot logistics window and safe network/area lifecycle operations. Format 70 persists an authoritative category mask for every storage provider. Boxes, chests and bulk bins now accept any OR combination of solid-goods categories through independent checkboxes, cannot disable their last category and never destroy already stored incompatible goods. Dispatcher selection, diagnostics, explicit-source compatibility, snapshots and hashing use the same rule. The default network remains canonical and automatically contains every adult not assigned elsewhere. Exact food/material variant filters remain later work.
- Stored and loose resource overviews default to a three-column, scrollable concrete-type layout. Dried rations, berries, mushrooms, roots, fish and raw meat use a dedicated transparent food atlas; woods, rocks, ores, gems and bars derive deterministic keyed thumbnails from their material palettes. Storage-detail contents aggregate identical concrete types into the same icon-and-count slots, and its title names and marks the concrete provider instead of saying only `Ustawienia składu`. Distinct plank and specialized bar/gem silhouettes remain later atlas work.
- Gameplay material and workshop definitions contain no translated display strings. The embedded translation kit has matching `en` and `pl` catalogs split into `interface`, `materials`, `recipes` and `workshops`, with subsection/key lookup and English fallback. Current Godot material and crafting-recipe labels use this catalog; the remaining older hardcoded interface strings still need incremental key migration and an options-language selector.
- Raised `SolidGround` and `Mud` columns now contain a mineable sandstone/granite volume below their material surface. Exposed fronts use ordinary pickaxe work, produce physical stone and become persistent traversable tunnels under the generator-v14 baseline.
- Generated terrain ramps can now be designated with ordinary rock mining on their lower/current level. Excavation leaves the lower floor in place, removes the climb edge, invalidates topology, produces stone and persists in the current exact save format. Intact ramps seen from one level above are deliberately brighter than other lower terrain.
- Mining candidates no longer rotate past the nearest feasible front. Gesture-start distance becomes the deterministic ordering tie-breaker for work cells, while linear walkways and walls persist as ordered segment groups and unlock delivery/building from the dragged start toward the end.
- Work-area selection has one authoritative target resolver shared by preview and command execution. Filtered gathering keeps only matching physical targets; excavation, scouting and cleaning keep applicable cells; ramp carving is single-cell; clearing orders apply to the whole selected region. Presentation style is centralized in `WorkToolCatalog`.
- Work-area commands issued while paused are applied without advancing simulation time, so their preview does not disappear before the planner can show the order.
- Cave levels materialize deterministically as the player reaches them. Generator v14 introduces basalt, obsidian, copper, silver, gold, gems, lava from depth 12 and broader lava bodies from depth 16. Hardness affects work duration; basalt requires building level 2, while obsidian requires building level 3 and a reinforced pickaxe. Mining creates physical, variant-preserving stone, ore and gem stacks.
- Mining-order readiness now distinguishes an unexposed tunnel front from missing Building knowledge, a missing pickaxe, insufficient building level and the obsidian-specific reinforced-pickaxe requirement. The cell inspector shows the same requirement before designation.
- Lazy excavation now gives deep fauna valid habitat in mutable tunnel cells: deep crawlers spawn from level 12 and cannot climb above it, while magma wyrms spawn from level 16 and remain below that boundary. Different species do not materialize on the same cell. Underground inspection lists visible creatures with maximum health and exact attack damage, as well as corpses and loose stacks on the selected cell.
- Lava is an explicit construction hazard rather than generic unsupported space. Wooden walkways remain valid over water and chasms but are rejected over lava. A separate gray basalt-walkway blueprint accepts lava, requests one physical basalt unit per segment and requires Building level 2 plus either pickaxe. Both variants use the same ordered construction, navigation and save/load rules; restored world objects are no longer capped at `z=-16`.
- Primitive workshops produce wooden buckets, barrels and reinforced pickaxes as physical equipment. A placed barrel is an ordinary water requester; bucket-equipped haulers carry water from reachable shallows into it, and thirsty goblins can drink from that stored supply.
- Raid camps release their assigned goblins after a completed raid, treat the bed count as the total occupant limit, prioritize the selected raid party over ordinary occupants and expose automatic healthiest/best-armed assignment plus visible readiness state. Corpse directives are generic rather than raid-only.
- Human villages now keep seed grain separately from edible food. New villages start with 12 grain; sowing a cleared, fully worked field consumes one grain and two stored water, while harvest returns two grain alongside food. Farmers prioritize sowable fields over emergency berry gathering, and field inspection names missing grain or water/bucket supply instead of showing an inert cleared plot.
- Generated food plants now retain the exact terrain-surface Z instead of being indexed as if every column lived at `z=0`. Ecology, loose brushwood and loose stone exclude natural ramp cells, and goblin forage routing uses the ordinary level-aware terrain graph, so generated gather targets are neither suspended in air nor placed on slopes.
- The full validation completes with 356/356 simulation tests passing, including network routing, lifecycle-command persistence, safe network deletion, guarded area resize, non-destructive area dissolution, compound container categories and exact format-70 save/load scenarios. The complete solution builds with zero warnings and zero errors.

## Deliberately unfinished

- Soil is still a thin cover over rock. Separate earth, clay or peat volumes, shovel work, unstable excavated earth walls and reinforcement are not implemented.
- Multi-cell player zones such as fields, larger stockpiles and dumping areas are specified as one object with one identity and a multi-cell footprint, but the existing primitive stockpiles have not yet been converted to that representation.
- The experimental 3D renderer remains parked beside the primary top-down 2D renderer. Fog, full materials and dynamic lighting are not reasons to block current 2D work.
- Deep-faction records activate as generated depth crosses their bands, but concrete dark-dwarf fortresses, inhabitants and expeditions are not materialized yet. Current deep enemies are depth-bound dangerous wildlife rather than an intelligent underground society.
- Generated lava and stable water bodies exist, but full fluid simulation, seepage, underground rivers, transfer volume, drainage, pumps, heat and terrain reactions remain design commitments rather than completed mechanics.
- Barrels currently provide one fixed water-storage blueprint and buckets one fixed hauling tool. Portable loose barrels, generic container capacities, fill/empty orders, water quality and explicit multi-source route policies remain later logistics slices.
- Completed constructions still discard the concrete material variant delivered to a generic wood/stone blueprint. Persist that provenance in the construction site and completed world object before palette-generating walls, furniture, fences, bridges, doors and sculptures from `materials.json`; migrate older saves to deterministic defaults.
- Metal-bar resource identities and validated smelting recipes exist, but smelter buildings, fuel/ore requesters, smelting jobs and physical bar production are not implemented yet. Crafting orders still reserve broad resource kinds, so variant-aware recipe inputs and material identity on finished equipment require a save-schema migration before “make this item from any compatible material” becomes playable.
- Raid aftermath now has persistent corpses, physical loot/recovery paths and dual-source corpse budding foundations, but concrete item identities throughout every inventory, player-selected loot destinations, pursuit/retreat policy, demolition and fire remain later raid slices.
